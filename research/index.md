# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-microscope" %}Research

**1. Viral Assembly & Antiviral Strategies**
We investigate the critical stage of the viral replication cycle: how hundreds of structural proteins interact with viral RNA to assemble into new infectious particles. By deciphering the structural and functional mechanics of these interactions, we aim to design targeted inhibitors that block viral assembly and halt infection. We focus on two major viral families:
 **Retroviruses:** We study RNA-to-DNA reverse-transcribing viruses, with a primary focus on HIV.
 **Flaviviruses:** We research severe global and regional pathogens, including the Dengue, Zika, and Tick-borne encephalitis (TBE) viruses.
 **Key Techniques:** To study these complex processes, we routinely employ a broad spectrum of molecular and cellular methods, including DNA/RNA isolation, molecular cloning, mutagenesis, tissue culture techniques, qPCR, Western Blotting (WB), Fast Protein Liquid Chromatography (FPLC), Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS), ELISA and CRISPR techniques.
**2. Advanced Biotechnology & Biotherapeutics**
 **Next-Generation Peptides:** We focus on the design and production of advanced peptide-based drugs and therapeutics.
 **Genetic Code Expansion:** We incorporate non-canonical amino acids (ncAAs) into target peptides to enhance their chemical diversity, stability, and therapeutic potential.
 **Cell-Free Protein Synthesis (CFPS):** To overcome the limitations of traditional _in vivo_ expression, we utilize highly controllable _in vitro_ CFPS platforms to synthesize these complex molecules efficiently.
